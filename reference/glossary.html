Glossary

Every technical term used in the lessons, defined once, used consistently. Grows as the course grows.

Study method

TermDefinition
Inductive studyDrawing conclusions out of the text by observation, rather than bringing conclusions to it. Three ordered steps: observation, interpretation, application.
ExpositionExplaining a passage's God-intended meaning in its context and pressing it on the hearer. "Expositional" study works through a book verse by verse rather than hopping between topics.
Grammatical-historical methodInterpreting a text by its grammar (what the words and sentences say) and its history (what the original author and audience meant and understood).
ContextThe material surrounding a passage: the paragraph, the section, the book, and the whole Bible. The first question of interpretation.
Cross-referenceAnother passage of Scripture that quotes, echoes, or explains the one you are studying. Scripture interprets Scripture.

The Gospels

TermDefinition
Gospel"Good news": the announcement of salvation through Jesus Christ. Also the literary form of the four books that narrate his life, death, and resurrection.
Synoptic GospelsMatthew, Mark, and Luke, which "see together": they share much material and order, unlike John. Parallel passages can sharpen what each author chose to include.
DiscourseAn extended block of Jesus' teaching. Matthew arranges five: the Sermon on the Mount (5-7), the Mission Discourse (10), the Kingdom Parables (13), the Community Discourse (18), and the Olivet Discourse (24-25). Each ends "when Jesus had finished these sayings".
Kingdom of heavenMatthew's signature phrase (32 times, nowhere else in Scripture): God's saving reign arriving in Jesus the King. Equivalent to "kingdom of God"; Matthew guards Jewish sensibilities about God's name.
Fulfillment formulaMatthew's repeated "this was to fulfill what was spoken by the prophet" (about a dozen times). His case that Jesus is the promised Messiah, quoting the OT over 60 times.
ParableA story from ordinary life that carries a kingdom meaning, revealing truth to the receptive and concealing it from the hardened (Matt 13:10-17).
Son of DavidA messianic title tracing Jesus' royal claim through David's line (Matt 1:1). Matthew uses it more than any other Gospel.
Son of ManJesus' favorite self-title, from Daniel 7:13-14: the human figure who receives everlasting dominion. Both lowly ("nowhere to lay his head") and glorious ("coming on the clouds").

Theology

TermDefinition
RepentanceA radical turning from sin to God, not mere regret, remorse, or a change of opinion. It is not a work that earns anything, but it always bears visible fruit (Matt 3:8). Scripture links it inseparably to faith: repentance turns from sin, faith turns to God. The first word of both John's preaching (3:2) and Jesus' (4:17).
Reformed theologyThe doctrinal stream of the Protestant Reformation: Scripture alone as final authority, salvation by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone, and God's absolute sovereignty in creation and salvation.
MessiahHebrew "anointed one" (Greek: Christ). The promised Davidic king. Matthew's whole Gospel argues that Jesus is he: the genealogy gives his credentials, the fulfillment quotations his prophetic warrant.
CovenantA formal bond God establishes with people, with promises and obligations. Matthew presents Jesus as heir of the Abrahamic and Davidic covenants (1:1) and mediator of the new covenant in his blood (26:28).
EschatologyThe doctrine of last things: how God brings history to its goal. The three Reformed positions on the millennium are premillennial, amillennial, and postmillennial (see the book map card).
PostmillennialismThe view that Christ returns after ("post") an age in which the gospel progressively triumphs among the nations, as the mustard seed and leaven parables picture (Matt 13:31-33). Our course's home lens (Durbin).
Preterism (partial)Reading certain judgment prophecies, especially much of Matthew 24, as fulfilled ("preter", past) in the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70, within the generation Jesus named (24:34), while the bodily second coming, resurrection, and final judgment remain future. Full preterism, which denies a future coming, is heresy; the partial form is the postmil home reading at the Olivet Discourse.
TypologyGod-designed patterns where earlier persons, events, or institutions (types) foreshadow and are fulfilled by later, greater realities (antitypes), supremely Christ: the true Israel (2:15), the greater Moses (5-7), the greater temple (12:6).
EcclesiologyThe doctrine of the church. Matthew is the only Gospel that uses the word "church" (16:18, 18:17), and the keys, discipline, and commission passages ground it.
